# 003. Simulation via Applied Kinematics

## 🔬 Conclusion: Is the Set Goal Physically Achievable?

This document steps beyond forensics (fraud detection) that diagnoses past anomalies, explaining the "Applied Kinematics" approach that simulates the "Ripple Effects" brought about by management decisions.
The conclusion here is the proof of: **"In the current system structure, are the goals (KPIs and destinations) set by management physically achievable, or are they merely 'fantasy goals' where calculations break down?"**

By using "Forward Kinematics (FK)," which calculates the chain reaction when funds are injected into a specific account, and "Inverse Kinematics (IK)," which reverse-calculates the necessary force from the goal, we reveal organizational bottlenecks and structural limits.

---

## Reverse Calculation from Goals and Physical Limits (3D Inverse Kinematics / IK)

By reverse-calculating from the desired "destination (goal)," we plot as 3D vectors what kind of force (funds/resources) should be applied to each node in the network.

### [Comparison 1] Natural and Efficient Optimal Route (Baseline)

![Sample 0 IK](../../samples/Sample_0_Healthy/readme_plots/003_1_2__3d_kinematics_ik.png)

* **Sample 0 (Healthy)**: Because the friction and viscosity of the system are low, the calculation engine (IK Solver) instantly finds the "most natural and gentle optimal route (short vectors)" to reach the goal. This indicates that achieving the goal is entirely possible with current organizational resources.

### [Comparison 2] Divergence and Breakdown of Calculation due to Deadlock (Kyoto Traffic)

![Sample 5 IK](../../samples/Sample_5_Kyoto_Traffic/readme_plots/003_1_2__3d_kinematics_ik.png)
*(※Sample 5: Pathfinding under chronic deadlock conditions)*

* **Sample 5 (Kyoto Traffic)**: When simulating arrival times at destinations in logistics or sales goals in finance, there are cases where all routes are blocked by "massive traffic jams (extremely high friction and stiffness)." In this case, the IK Solver cannot reach the goal and either diverges into astronomically massive abnormal values (vectors) or fails the calculation itself (error). This mathematically proves that **"with current organizational resources and processes, the KPIs set by management are physically unachievable."**

## Ripple Effects and Unexpected Bottlenecks (3D Forward Kinematics / FK)

After confirming the feasibility of achieving the goal (macro), we use Forward Kinematics to simulate what kind of "ripple effect" occurs at the edges of the organization (micro) when funds (force) are injected into a specific department.

### Detection of Unexpected Spikes

![Sample 0 FK](../../samples/Sample_0_Healthy/readme_plots/003_1_1__3d_kinematics_fk.png)
*(Above image: Normal chain reaction in Sample 0)*

* **📊 Visual Structure**: A 3D spatial plot showing how much other accounts move (react) when a force (funds) is applied to a specific account.
* **🚨 Anomaly Detection**: In a normal organization (Sample 0), an investment in the sales department gently pushes up the sales and cash accounts. However, if the organization's internal processes are fragile, unexpectedly massive and violent displacements (sharp spikes) are triggered in nodes that should originally be unrelated, such as "specific expense accounts" or "accounts payable (obligations)." This serves as an advance warning of the existence of bottlenecks that cannot handle the speed of growth.

## 🔬 Falsifiability and Model Limits (Application to Practice)

Kinematic simulation asserts the **mathematical consequence** that "assuming the current network structure (spring strength and friction), an infinite amount of force is required to achieve this goal (physically impossible)."
However, that calculation is strictly based on the "current structure." If a "radical reform of business processes (introduction of new APIs or personnel restructuring)" is conducted, the spring structure itself changes, and previously impossible goals might become achievable.

When receiving the result that the IK solver diverged (calculation failure), management consultants and managers should not "put unreasonable pressure on the field," but should transition to a fundamental discussion (additional verification) on "how the current organizational structure (topology) itself must be rebuilt to achieve the goal."
